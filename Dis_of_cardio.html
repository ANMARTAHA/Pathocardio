Overview

The rules and figures worth remembering — not the whole lecture.

Homeostasis & fluid

  • Total body water ≈ 60% of body weight.
  • Intracellular fluid = 33%; extracellular fluid = 27%.
  • ICF: low Na⁺/Cl⁻. ECF: high Na⁺, Cl⁻, HCO₃⁻.
  • Plasma has more protein than interstitial fluid.

Hyperemia vs congestion

  • Active hyperemia = ↑ arterial inflow (arteriolar dilation).
  • Passive hyperemia / congestion = ↓ venous outflow.
  • Causes of active hyperemia: neurogenic, fever, inflammation, blushing, menopausal flush, exercise, goiter, AV malformation.
  • Congestion → ↓O₂, ↑metabolic waste, cyanosis (deoxy-Hb).
  • Local congestion e.g. portal obstruction in cirrhosis; systemic e.g. heart failure, lung disease.

Oedema — 6 mechanisms

  • 1. ↑ Hydrostatic pressure (local: DVT; generalized: CHF).
  • 2. ↓ Plasma oncotic pressure (albumin loss — nephrotic syndrome).
  • 3. Lymphatic obstruction (filariasis, carcinoma / node dissection).
  • 4. Na⁺ & water retention (intrinsic renal, aldosterone, ADH).
  • 5. Inflammation → protein-rich exudate, SG 1.015–1.020.
  • 6. Drugs: NSAIDs, Ca²⁺ blockers, corticosteroids, antidiabetics.
  • Pitting = subcutaneous; non-pitting = lymphoedema.

Virchow’s triad

  • 1. Endothelial injury — key for arterial & cardiac thrombi.
  • 2. Abnormal blood flow — turbulence (arterial) / stasis (venous).
  • 3. Hypercoagulability — hereditary (AT-III, protein C/S deficiency) or acquired (age, immobility, smoking, malignancy, pregnancy).
  • Arterial thrombi grow retrograde; venous thrombi extend with flow — both propagate toward the heart.

Fate of a thrombus

  • Propagation — accumulates platelets/fibrin, obstructs vessel.
  • Embolization — dislodges, travels elsewhere.
  • Dissolution — fibrinolysis (± streptokinase/urokinase, best early).
  • Organization & recanalization — fibrosis, may reopen a channel.

Embolism — 99% are thrombi

  • Types: thrombo-, fat/marrow, bacterial, air, amniotic fluid, tumor cell, foreign body.
  • Fat embolism: after long-bone fracture; seen in 90%, symptomatic in <10%.
  • Air embolism: needs >100 cc; "the bends/chokes."
  • Amniotic fluid embolism: 1/50,000 deliveries, 80% mortality, causes DIC.
  • Venous emboli → pulmonary embolism (most common & fatal form).

Infarction

  • Ischemic necrosis; 99% thrombotic/embolic.
  • White (anemic): arterial occlusion, solid single-supply organs (heart, spleen, kidney), pyramid-shaped coagulative necrosis.
  • Red (haemorrhagic): venous occlusion or dual-supply loose organs (lung, liver, gut, brain).
  • Septic: infected vegetation or necrotic tissue seeding.
  • Neurons tolerate ischemia 3–4 min; myocardium 20–30 min.

Coronary artery disease & MI

  • Plaque = lipid core (foam cells) + fibrous cap (smooth muscle cells).
  • Rupture → luminal thrombus over necrotic core.
  • MI diagnosis: ↑/↓ troponin plus one of — ischemic symptoms, new ST-T change/LBBB, pathological Q wave, imaging loss of viable myocardium, intracoronary thrombus on angiography/autopsy.
  • Risk factors: smoking, cholesterol, hypertension, inactivity, obesity, diabetes, alcohol, stress, diet, age.

Pulmonary oedema

  • Cardiogenic: ↑ pulmonary wedge pressure → rapid transudation.
  • Non-cardiogenic: damaged alveolar-capillary membrane leaks protein + fluid, wedge pressure normal.
  • Hypoalbuminemia does NOT cause pulmonary oedema.
  • Causes: ARDS, re-expansion after pneumothorax/large pleural tap, narcotic overdose (heroin, methadone).
